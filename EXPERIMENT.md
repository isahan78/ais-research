## 1. Summary

We cut a reasoning model's thinking trace at several points and, at each cut, race two predictors of whether the final answer will be correct: a probe reading the model's internal activations, and the strongest predictor we can build that reads only the trace text. The gap between them tells us whether internals genuinely know something the written reasoning doesn't — and if the gap is near zero, monitoring the text is all anyone needed.

## 2. Why

Several 2025–26 papers report that linear probes on mid-trace activations predict a reasoning model's final outcome well before it is stated — feeding the narrative "the model has already decided; the rest is theater," and the safety hope that internals can be monitored early.

Two published positions now disagree about what those probes are actually reading:

- **Camp Internals:** [Can We Predict Alignment Before Models Finish Thinking?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12428) — activation probes beat capable LLMs and fine-tuned *text* classifiers by ~13 F1, attributed to "performative CoTs" whose text actively misleads.
- **Camp Text:** [Current activation oracles are hard to use](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LXQBcztrWKhtcgQfJ/current-activation-oracles-are-hard-to-use) — after filtering cases an LLM could answer from the preceding text alone, ~95% of apparent internal signal vanished (~100/2,300 survived). See also [Linear probes rely on textual evidence](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21344v2).

Both cannot be generally true. Nobody has mapped where the line sits. This experiment measures the quantity they disagree about, on a domain (math correctness) neither covers, and pre-registers both camps' predictions before running.

Stakes: CoT monitoring is a live safety strategy (Nanda: ["CoT monitoring is actually pretty great… should be widely used"](https://x.com/NeelNanda5/status/1941826045260484934)). If Δ≈0, text monitoring is vindicated as sufficient. If Δ>0 somewhere, that regime is exactly where text monitoring needs internal backup.

## 3. The measurement

**Prediction target:** will *this specific run* end in a correct final answer? (Not "does the model know this problem" — the probe and the text reader both watch this one trace; label and trace are the same event, so single-sample labels are the correct target, not noise.)

For each truncation point k:
- `S_probe(k)` — held-out ROC-AUC of a linear probe on internal activations at the cut.
- `S_text(k)` — max over the text-only predictors on the identical prefix.
- **`Δ(k) = S_probe(k) − S_text(k)`** — the experiment, as one curve.

## 4. Data — five layers

| Layer | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problems | ~300 questions from **MMLU-Pro** (10-option multiple choice). Chosen by *measurement*, not assumption — see Decision Log 2026-08-26 | [MMLU-Pro](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TIGER-Lab/MMLU-Pro), 12,032 test items, ungated, auto-gradable (single letter in `\boxed{}`) |
| 2. Traces | One generation per problem: full thinking text + final answer. Thinking budget raised (≥8k tokens) so truncation exclusions stay rare — capped budgets delete the struggling (= incorrect) traces and bias the sample (survivor bias) | Generated by us: Qwen3-8B, thinking mode |
| 3. Cuts | Freeze each trace at k ∈ {10, 25, 50, 75, 90}% of thinking tokens → ~300 rows of (problem, cut, prefix, label) | Derived |
| 4. Two views per cut | **Scan:** residual-stream vectors at 3 depths (layers 9/18/27), last prefix token. **Page:** the identical prefix as text | Derived; both racers see the same information horizon — the symmetry that makes Δ meaningful |
| 5. Split | Train/test **by problem** (~40/20). Every cut of a problem lives on one side, or the probe cheats by recognizing problems | Derived |

Nothing human, scraped, private, or gated. One public benchmark + our own generated behavior. Fully reproducible for ~$2 of GPU.

## 5. The racers

**Probe:** logistic regression on activations. Deliberately simple — the linear probe *is* the claim under audit.

**Text side — take the max; a weak baseline inflates Δ for free, so tuning these hard is the integrity of the result:**
1. **LLM judge** — frontier model reads the prefix, predicts the outcome (Nanda's named "ask an LLM" baseline).
2. **Trained text classifier** — same training split as the probe; genuinely tuned, not a strawman.
3. **Forced-answer** — the subject model itself, interrupted mid-thought and made to answer immediately. On-policy, nearly free, appears in none of the audited papers' abstracts; our most novel component. If this matches the probe, the conclusion sharpens: you don't need probes — *you can just ask.*
4. (Gate-1 crude floor: prefix token count + problem level, ~10 lines, no GPU.)

## 6. Analyses — three readings of the same data

1. **Vertical:** Δ(k) at each cut, bootstrap CIs.
2. **Horizontal:** token-lead — "the probe at 25% knows what the text reader only knows at 75%." Same curves, read sideways; the quotable framing.
3. **The performative cut (adjudication):** split test cases by whether the best text reader was right vs. confident-but-wrong. Camp Internals predicts Δ>0 concentrated in the misled cases; Camp Text predicts Δ≈0 everywhere. No new data — a sort of data we already have.

## 7. Controls and known traps

- **Split by problem, never by cut** — else probes memorize problems (this alone could fabricate the published effect).
- **ROC-AUC, never accuracy** — base-rate cheating.
- **Shuffled-label floor** (enough seeds to resolve a p95) — "beats noise" is the minimum bar, not the result.
- **Multiple-comparisons honesty** — best-of-3-layers must be compared against a floor of the same max-statistic, or corrected.
- **Length/position control** — within-k analysis only; check probe on length-matched subsets (a probe reading "how far along am I" is not reading the answer).
- **Prompt alignment** — activations must come from the byte-identical prefix the trace was generated from.
- **Information parity** — probe and text classifier train on the same examples.
- **Agent-sanity rule** — every reported number traceable to the code that produced it; 10 random examples hand-checked per stage (checklist in README.md).

## 8. Outcomes and decision rules

| Result | Meaning | Reportable as |
|---|---|---|
| Early prediction doesn't reproduce | The claim fails on math correctness at this scale | High-quality failed replication (Nanda, verbatim: replications and negative results "are all very valuable contributions. I would personally consider these novel") |
| Reproduces; Δ≈0 | Probes were re-reading the page | Leakage calibration the field needs; text monitoring vindicated |
| Δ>0 in some regime | Internals add signal — measured, located | Positive finding; test the performative-cut prediction |

## 9. Claims calibration

n≈60 → wide CIs. Verbs scale with evidence: "suggestive evidence that…" / "in our setting…" / "we found at least one regime where…". Never "models know their answers early is false." Overclaiming fails in front of exactly this reader.

## 10. Known limitations (stated in advance)

One model (Qwen3-8B) · one domain (MMLU-Pro; competition math measured and rejected, Decision Log 2026-08-26) · n=300, **n_test=102 per cut — all CIs wide** · linear probes only · six-point k grid · correctness ≠ alignment (2507.12428 tested alignment) · **Δ is an upper bound until the LLM judge runs** · probe AUC non-monotonic in k (0.761 → 0.647 → 0.681), most likely small-n noise, not reported as a trend · k=1 forced-answer missing (OOM).

## 12. Pre-registration — SEALED 2026-08-29, BEFORE Block 2. NEVER EDIT.

> Written before any Block-2 data existed. Git history is the tamper-evidence (commit timestamp vs. run timestamps in RESULTS.md). Post-hoc analyses are permitted but must be labelled post-hoc in the write-up.
> Two independent forecasts were recorded — the owner's and Tyler's — without either seeing the other's first. Where they disagree is where this experiment carries the most information.

**AUC scale:** 0.5 = chance · 0.7 = decent · 0.9 = strong.

| Quantity | Owner (Isahan) | Tyler | Agree? |
|---|---|---|---|
| S_probe at k=50 (best layer) | **0.70** | **0.72** | ✅ close |
| S_text at k=50 (best reader) | **0.60** | **0.70** | ❌ **disagree** |
| **Δ at k=50** | **+0.10** | **+0.02** | ❌ **the crux** |
| Δ at k=10 | ≈ 0 (tied) | small positive, ~+0.03 | ~agree |
| Performative cut (text confidently wrong) | ~70% confident Camp Internals is right | Camp Internals directionally right (Δ ≈ +0.08 there) but **n too small to resolve** | partial |
| MMLU-Pro failure rate (n=300) | **40%** | **23%** (±5) — matching Run 004's measurement | ❌ disagree |

**The crux, stated plainly.** Both of us expect the probe to land near 0.70. We disagree about the *text*. Owner bets the text reader is materially weaker (0.60), leaving a real gap. Tyler bets that by the halfway point of a multiple-choice trace the model has usually already written something like "so it's probably C", that a tuned TF-IDF classifier and a frontier judge will both read that, and that the on-policy forced-answer baseline will be strongest of all — leaving almost no gap.

**So: Owner leans Camp Internals. Tyler leans Camp Text.** Δ ≈ +0.10 vs Δ ≈ +0.02 is the bet, and the experiment settles it.

**Falsification, pre-committed:** if Δ at k=50 has a bootstrap CI overlapping 0, the honest headline is *"the probe's apparent early-prediction advantage does not survive strong text baselines in this setting"* — regardless of which of us wanted otherwise. If Δ is clearly positive, Tyler was wrong and the write-up says so.

**Also pre-committed:** the text baseline is scored as the **max** over all available readers (forced-answer, frontier judge, tuned TF-IDF, crude floor). We do not get to pick the weakest one after seeing results.
